Module 4: Point-and-Draw Devices vs. Graphics Digitizers

Comparing coordinate tracking mechanisms and pressure sensitivity for CAD and digital art applications.

Point-and-Draw (Mouse & Trackball)

Relative delta tracking $(dx, dy)$ via optical CMOS sensors. Unmatched for orthogonal vector snapping, grid alignment, and CAD toolbars. Binary click feedback (0 pressure levels).

Graphics Digitizers & Tablets

Direct absolute $(X, Y)$ mapping via electromagnetic resonance (EMR) grids. Captures 8,192+ levels of pressure sensitivity and barrel tilt angle, translating hand muscle tension into organic stroke dynamics.

Module 5: Low-Level vs. High-Level Programming Languages

Distinguish between Machine, Assembly, and High-Level languages in execution speed, hardware dependence, and translation.

Machine Language

Pure binary / hex opcodes executed directly by CPU circuitry. Zero translation overhead, but unreadable and architecture-tied.

Assembly Language

Symbolic mnemonics (MOV, ADD) mapped 1:1 to machine instructions. Requires an Assembler. Used in OS kernels and embedded firmware.

High-Level Languages

English-like syntax (Python, C++, Java) with memory management and platform independence. Translated via compilers or interpreters.
